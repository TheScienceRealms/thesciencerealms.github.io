## Accepted observations (exact wording matters)
Conventions: "/" means either answer is accepted (e.g. yellow/orange, but NOT "yellow-orange"). "-" means both colours are needed (red-brown; "red" or "brown" alone is wrong). "Make a solution" means dissolve in water.

- **Halogens:**
  - F₂ yellow gas
  - Cl₂ green/yellow-green/green-yellow gas
  - Br₂ red-brown liquid (and vapour)
  - I₂ grey-black solid, violet/purple vapour
- **In water and solvents:**
  - chlorine water green/colourless
  - bromine water yellow/orange/brown
  - iodine solution yellow/brown in polar solvents, violet/purple in non-polar solvents
- **Solid halides with concentrated H₂SO₄:**
  - F⁻ and Cl⁻ give steamy/misty fumes
  - Br⁻ gives steamy/misty fumes plus red-brown vapour
  - I⁻ gives steamy/misty fumes, violet/purple vapour, rotten egg smell (H₂S), yellow solid (S) and grey-black solid (I₂)
- **Solid halides with concentrated H₃PO₄:** steamy/misty fumes only
- **Indicators:**
  - methyl orange: acid into alkali yellow to red; alkali into acid red to yellow
  - phenolphthalein: acid into alkali pink to colourless; alkali into acid colourless to pink
- **Gas tests:**
  - H₂: pop with a burning splint
  - O₂: relights a glowing splint
  - Cl₂: bleaches damp litmus/UI paper
  - CO₂: limewater turns milky
  - HCl: white fumes/smoke with a glass rod or stopper dipped in CONCENTRATED ammonia
  - NH₃: the same, with concentrated HCl
- **Flame colours:**
  - Li⁺ crimson
  - Na⁺ yellow/orange
  - K⁺ lilac (pink through cobalt glass)
  - Ca²⁺ brick red/red
  - Ba²⁺ green/apple green
  - Cu²⁺ green-blue/blue-green
- **Ion tests:**
  - NH₄⁺: warm with NaOH(aq), pungent smell
  - SO₄²⁻: make a solution, add BaCl₂ or Ba(NO₃)₂ solution, white precipitate
  - Halide ions: dissolve in dilute HNO₃, add AgNO₃ solution
    - Cl⁻ white ppt, soluble in dilute NH₃
    - Br⁻ cream ppt, soluble in concentrated NH₃
    - I⁻ yellow ppt, insoluble in concentrated NH₃
  - Carbonate: dilute acid gives a colourless gas that turns limewater milky
  - Iodine: add starch, blue-black
- **AS2 observations:**
  - Burning hydrocarbons: smoky/sooty flame means high carbon content or incomplete combustion
  - Alkene test: bromine water decolourises after shaking
  - Burning alcohols: clean blue flame
  - Alcohol + Na: solid disappears, fizzing, warms
  - Alcohol + PCl₅: solid disappears, steamy/misty fumes, warms, hissing
  - Oxidising primary/secondary alcohols: acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ orange to green when heated, change in smell
- **Group II:**
  - With water: Mg gives a few bubbles slowly and the metal dulls; Ca fizzes, warms, rises and falls, disappears and gives a white solid
  - With HCl(aq): disappears, fizzing, warms
  - With H₂SO₄: Ca fizzes initially, then stops (insoluble CaSO₄)
  - With O₂: Mg burns with a bright white light giving a white powder; Ca burns with a brick red/red flame giving a white powder

## Chief Examiner's pet hates (common lost marks)
- **Wording:**
  - "solid" is not "precipitate" (ppt is allowed)
  - "clear" is not "colourless"
  - "cloudy" is not "milky"
  - "misty fumes" is not credited for the HCl/NH₃ gas test (white fumes)
  - "dative" alone is not accepted (co-ordinate or dative covalent is)
  - "simple molecular" is not accepted (molecular covalent is)
  - "permanganate" is wrong (manganate(VII))
- **Charges and oxidation states:** charges are written 2+, not +2. Oxidation states are written +2.
- **Names and configurations:**
  - give systematic names with oxidation states
  - write electron configurations in principal quantum number order (3d before 4s), with lower-case subshells
- **Forces:** always say "between molecules", and explain boiling point trends by number of electrons (not mass)
- **Units and expressions:**
  - °C with the degree symbol
  - unit symbols, not words
  - table headings as quantity / unit
  - + sign on positive ΔH
  - Kc expressions start with "Kc =" and use square brackets
- **Organic:**
  - no over-condensed alkyl groups like C₃H₇ or C₄H₉OH
  - bracket the middle substituents: CH₃CH(OH)CH₃
  - connectivity: HO–C, H₂N–C, HOOC–C
  - curly arrows start at a bond or lone pair; include the carbocation and the leaving halide ion
  - use wedges and dashes for 3D
- **Calculations:**
  - label each step
  - convert cm³ to dm³
  - use the mole ratio
  - give the requested precision
  - in calorimetry, carry on to moles and ΔH with its sign
- **Practicals:**
  - name key apparatus (volumetric flask, separating funnel, pear-shaped flask, still head)
  - reflux prevents loss of volatile reactants and products
  - water enters the condenser at the bottom
  - the thermometer bulb is level with the side arm
  - anti-bumping granules give smooth boiling
  - "heat slowly" for melting point, and record the temperature at start and end
  - drying agents must be anhydrous (and not anhydrous CuSO₄ in that question)
  - masses to 2 decimal places in g; temperatures as whole numbers in °C
  - the NH₄⁺ test needs warming with NaOH solution
- **Catalysts:** "lowers activation energy" alone was not credited. The answer needs "provides an alternative route with lower activation energy".

## Key definitions (mark-scheme wording)
- **Relative atomic mass:** the average (weighted mean) mass of an atom of an element relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12
- **Disproportionation:** oxidation and reduction of the same element in the same reaction. Answers must name the element, e.g. chlorine.
- **Hydrated salt:** a salt which contains water of crystallisation
- **Weak acid/base:** partially dissociates in water
- **Metallic bonding:** attraction between positive ions and delocalised electrons
- **Molecular formula:** a formula which shows the actual number of atoms of each element in a molecule
- **Structural isomers:** molecules which have the same molecular formula but a different structural formula
- **Average bond enthalpy:** the energy required to break one mole of a given bond, averaged over many compounds
- **Standard enthalpy of neutralisation:** the enthalpy change when one mole of water is produced in a neutralisation reaction under standard conditions
- **Dynamic equilibrium:** the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction, and the amounts of reactants and products remain constant
- **Homogeneous reaction:** all the reactants and products are in the same physical state
- **Solubility:** the maximum mass of solute that will dissolve in 100 g of solvent at a stated temperature
- **Maxwell–Boltzmann:** the y-axis is number of molecules, and no molecules have zero energy
- **E/Z isomerism:** restricted rotation about the C=C, plus different atoms or groups on each carbon of the C=C. The Z isomer has both higher-priority groups on the same side.
- **Nucleophile:** an ion or molecule with a lone pair of electrons that it donates

## Recurring exam content (2022–2026)
- **AS1, every year:**
  - Group VII halogens: observations, conc. H₂SO₄ reactions, disproportionation with NaOH
  - a hydrated salt or weak acid titration to find x or Mr (oxalic acid 2022, tartaric acid 2024, M₂CO₃ 2025, Na₂CO₃·xH₂O 2026)
  - bonding and structure (giant ionic, metallic, molecular covalent, graphite)
  - ionisation energies and electron configurations
  - redox with half-equations and oxidation states
  - dot and cross diagrams (H₃O⁺, Na₂O, peroxide)
  - shapes and bond angles
  - a QWC question (e.g. mass spectrum of Cl₂ in 2026: peaks at 35, 37, 70, 72, 74)
- **AS2, every year:**
  - Group II (solubility of hydroxides and sulfates, reactions, configurations, reactivity explained by shielding)
  - energetics (calorimetry, bond enthalpies, Hess's law, enthalpy of formation)
  - equilibrium (Le Chatelier, Kc expression)
  - radical substitution (initiation, propagation, termination)
  - halogenoalkane nucleophilic substitution mechanism
  - isomerism (structural, E/Z with priorities)
  - alcohol oxidation, reflux vs distillation and IR (QWC)
  - Maxwell–Boltzmann and catalysts
  - empirical and molecular formula
  - industrial processes (Contact, Solvay)
