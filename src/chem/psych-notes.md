# The Psycheverse: WJEC AS Psychology notes

## Unit 1: Psychology, Past to Present (The Theoretical Frontier)
1 h 30 min exam. 50% of AS. AO1 25%, AO2 6.25%, AO3 18.75% (of AS).

For each of the five approaches (biological, psychodynamic, behaviourist, cognitive, positive), students must:
- know the assumptions
- apply ONE assumption to explain the formation of a relationship
- know one therapy and its main components
- evaluate the therapy (effectiveness and ethics)
- evaluate the approach (strengths, weaknesses, comparison with the other four)
- know and evaluate the classic research: methodology, procedures, findings, conclusions, ethical issues, social implications

| Approach | Assumptions | Therapy (one) | Classic research |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biological | evolutionary influences; localisation of brain function; neurotransmitters | drug therapy OR psychosurgery | Raine, Buchsbaum & LaCasse (1997), PET scans of murderers |
| Psychodynamic | influence of childhood experiences; the unconscious mind; tripartite personality | dream analysis OR group psychotherapy | Bowlby (1944), Forty-four juvenile thieves |
| Behaviourist | blank slate; behaviour learnt through conditioning; humans and animals learn in similar ways | aversion therapy OR systematic desensitisation | Watson & Rayner (1920), Little Albert |
| Cognitive | computer analogy; internal mental processes; schemas | CBT OR rational emotive behaviour therapy | Loftus & Palmer (1974), car crash leading questions |
| Positive | acknowledgement of free will; authenticity of goodness and excellence; focus on "the good life" | mindfulness OR quality of life therapy | Myers & Diener (1995), Who is happy? |

## Unit 2: Using Psychological Concepts (The Research Frontier)
1 h 30 min exam. 50% of AS. AO1 15%, AO2 23.75%, AO3 11.25% (of AS).

**Section A: Contemporary debates** (both sides; ethical, economic and social implications; use the five approaches; Welsh research where possible)
- the ethics of neuroscience
- the mother as primary care-giver of an infant
- using conditioning techniques to control the behaviour of children
- reliability of eye-witness testimony (including children)
- relevance of positive psychology in today's society

**Section B: Principles of research and application to a novel scenario**
- **Classic studies:** Milgram (1963) obedience (social psychology); Kohlberg (1968), The child as a moral philosopher (developmental psychology)
- **Research question:** aim; research, alternative (experimental), directional, non-directional and null hypotheses; IV, DV, co-variables, operationalisation, confounding and extraneous variables
- **Methodologies:** experiments; quasi-experiments (including natural); participant and non-participant observation; content analysis; structured interviews/questionnaires; semi-structured interviews; correlational studies; case studies; self-reports; quantitative and qualitative data; primary and secondary sources
- **Location:** laboratory, field, online
- **Participants:** target population, sampling frame, random, opportunity, systematic, stratified, quota, self-selected and snowball sampling; observational sampling (event, time)
- **Experimental design:** independent groups, repeated measures, matched pairs
- **Levels of measurement:** nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio
- **Graphs:** frequency tables, line graphs, histograms, bar charts, pie charts, scatter diagrams
- **Descriptive statistics:** mean, median, mode; range; standard deviation √(Σ(x−x̄)²/(n−1))
- **Reliability:** internal, external, ways of dealing with it
- **Validity:** internal, external; researcher bias, demand characteristics, social desirability; ways of dealing with it
- **Ethics:** confidentiality; deception; risk of stress, anxiety, humiliation or pain; risk to values, beliefs, relationships, status or privacy; valid consent; vulnerable individuals (including children); animals; ethics committees, guidelines, debriefing
- **Maths:** Appendix A. Skills in bold are Unit 4 only. Inferential tests are not in Unit 2.

## Sample assessment materials (wjec-gce-psychology-sams-from-2015.pdf)
Extracted text: ../wjec-sams/sams.txt. Unit 1 and Unit 2 mark scheme text: psych-sams-ms12.txt

**Unit 1 specimen paper (no sections, 80 marks)**
- Describe an assumption [3+3]
- Explain how a relationship is formed using two approaches [10, 5 per approach]
- Describe the main components of a therapy [8], then evaluate it [10]
- Findings and conclusions of a classic study [10]
- Strengths and weaknesses of an approach [10]
- Compare and contrast two approaches and their therapies in a client scenario [10]
- Ethical issues of a classic study with reference to its procedures [16]

**Unit 2 specimen paper**
- Section A: one debate essay [20], e.g. "Neuroscience… ethical implications are debatable. Discuss."
- Section B:
  - Short definitions [2], e.g. social desirability
  - Findings and conclusions of Milgram [8]
  - Identify a design or hypothesis [1+1]
  - Two ways to assess validity [2]: face, content, concurrent, predictive, construct
  - Novel scenario 1 (water and maths test, matched pairs): confounding variable [2]; directional hypothesis [2]; identify design [1]; advantage [3]; draw graph [6]; analyse ethics [8]
  - Novel scenario 2 (first-time mothers, questionnaire, self-selected via posters): modal scores [2]; standard deviation [5]; validity issues [4] and improvements [4]; alternative sampling [3]; advantage and disadvantage of primary data [3+3]

**How WJEC marks**
- Mostly banded levels of response: well detailed, accurate and effective terminology at the top, down to superficial at the bottom.
- Start at the top of a band. Mark positively.
- Indicative content is not prescriptive. Other appropriate material is credited.

**Specific marking rules**
- **Relationships:** the two explanations must come from two DIFFERENT approaches.
- **Directional hypothesis (2 marks):** must be operationalised and in context, e.g. "Students who drink water through the school day will score higher on a maths test than students who do not". "Condition A will do better than B" gets only 1. A non-directional hypothesis gets 0.
- **Confounding variable (2 marks):** must be linked to the study context.
- **Design advantage:** "quick" is not credited. It must be applied to the study for 3 marks.
- **Graph (6 marks):** title, x-axis label, y-axis label, suitable scale, accurate plotting (2).
- **Standard deviation (5 marks):** one each for the mean, (x − x̄), (x − x̄)², the total and the n − 1 denominator. Sample answer 1.15 (Σ = 10.4, n − 1 = 9).
- **Classic studies:** only details from the ORIGINAL article are credited (noted for Milgram and Watson & Rayner).
- **Loftus & Palmer findings:** smashed 40.8 mph, collided 39.3, bumped 38.1, hit 34.0, contacted 31.8. Broken glass "yes": smashed 16, hit 7, control 6 (out of 50 each).
- **Repeated measures** is also accepted as related measures, related samples or within groups. **Null hypothesis** is also accepted as H0.
